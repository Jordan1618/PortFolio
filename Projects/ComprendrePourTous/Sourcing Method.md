# **1) What I wanted to do :**

The sources are the heart of the project. A guide on health and relationships is only worth something if a reader can check every figure. I wanted a method where each claim has a real source that can be opened, and where a missing source is shown, not hidden. This note explains the method and the real cases where I refused or replaced a doubtful source.

# **2) The rules :**

- **One real source per sub-part.** Every sub-part of every chapter carries at least one real, verifiable scientific or official source. This was done guide by guide on 7 and 12 August (for example 54 sources for 54 sub-parts in Pour Nous, 131 sources for 120 sub-parts in Pour Elle).
- **The link goes on the sentence.** The hyperlink sits directly on the sentence the source supports, with no bare "(source: ...)" tag. Rule: one claim per link, about 25 words at most, no semicolon in the linked text. A link carrying three claims is a translated study summary pasted in, and must be cut.
- **Write the sentence, then source it.** Never the reverse. I learned this the hard way: when the sentence is built from the source's abstract, all the content ends up inside the brackets and only glue remains outside.
- **Dated and attributed.** The author, the journal and a verification date appear in the sources section, not in the body.
- **Reciprocity.** Every source linked in a chapter must also be in the central page `4 - Sources/<guide>.md`, with the same direct link, and the other way round. A script counts the links without a match.
- **Never fabricate a reference, a URL or a DOI.** A guessed DOI points to another article and the error is invisible. A full reference without a link is better than a link that points elsewhere.
- **Say when nothing is found.** If nothing verifiable is found after a serious search, the text says it, and the claim is listed in a section "claims without an identified precise source". Silence is not allowed.
- **Real testimonies only.** A published testimony with a link, cited like a scientific source. Never invented, even "as an illustration".
- **Two research rounds in deep mode:** one for the phenomenon and the object itself, one for the mechanism, a limit or counter-study, a concrete case, and an angle nobody asked for.
- **Dates use the real system date** of the day, not a date seen elsewhere in the conversation.

# **3) Real cases where I refused or replaced a source :**

- **A testimony that does not exist (STI guide).** For the chapters on chlamydia, gonorrhoea, trichomoniasis and Mycoplasma genitalium, no published and verifiable testimony about the specific infection was found. The chapters say so in plain words ("this is stated here explicitly rather than filled with an invention") and the claim is also listed as "without an identified precise source". A made-up testimony would have been easier and more moving, and was refused.
- **Generic search links as sources.** The 61 entries of the Pour Elle sources page pointed to a generic Google Scholar search, not to a real article. I replaced them with the direct link actually used in the chapter (DOI, PubMed, ACOG, NICE and so on) and added the sources that were used in chapters but missing from the page. The same was done for the STI guide. In the audit, one guide had 21% of its links without reciprocity because of placeholders never replaced.
- **Wrong Wikipedia links.** On 7 August I asked to check all the Wikipedia links that were not correct, and to make every cross-reference clickable.
- **Weak sources on central claims.** The audit flagged wellness blogs and commercial test sites used for core statements. In Pour Nous chapter 14, the weak sources on infidelity and domestic violence were replaced by primary or official sources.
- **A source cited but never explained.** In the personality guide, studies were cited without saying what the researchers did. This was not a missing source but a missing explanation, and it led to the new writing skill.
- **Reciprocity restored by script.** After the rework, Pour Nous went from 48 missing links to 0 out of 128, and Psychologie de la personnalité reached 142 out of 142.

# **4) A small case of a source that is honest by nature :**

In the nutrition guide, vitamin B12 is described as the strictest case "because no reliable plant source exists". The claim is linked to a source that says plants do not provide active B12. It is a good example of a sentence that states the limit of the evidence instead of smoothing it.

# **5) What I learned :**

- A rule that can be counted (a link without a match, a sub-part without a source) is applied. A rule about quality alone is forgotten
- Showing the gap ("no testimony found") builds more trust than filling it
- Source links must be real and direct, and checked by a script at every build
- Citing a study is not explaining it: the reader must know what was done, what came out, and why it matters
- Sourcing is the part of AI-assisted writing where human control matters most, because an AI can write a plausible reference that does not exist
