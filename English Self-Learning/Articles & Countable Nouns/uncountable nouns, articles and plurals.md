# uncountable nouns, articles and plurals

## Rule
- No a before fluent English: I speak fluent English.
- documentation, knowledge, advice and information are uncountable: no -s, no a. Use each new document, or each new piece of documentation.
- A few and some need a plural noun: a few examples, some examples.
- A person is singular: the person who knows, not the men who knows.

## Cousins (if relevant)
| Wrong | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a fluent English | fluent English | no article here |
| each new documentation | each new document | documentation is uncountable |
| A few example | A few examples | few needs a plural |
| The men who knows | The person who knows | one subject, one verb form |

## The trap
In French, documentation can be counted (une documentation) and the plural s on nouns is silent, so it is easy to forget. English is stricter on countable and uncountable nouns.

## Rule of thumb
Before a or a number, ask: can I count it? If not, no a and no -s.
